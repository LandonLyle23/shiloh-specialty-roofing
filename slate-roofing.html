Slate Roofing

Natural Slate

Slate: the roof, and the roof underneath it, need to last the same amount of time

Quarried slate is graded largely by thickness and consistency. S1 grade is rated for 75 to 100+ years and is what's specified on a genuine restoration or high-end build. Slate is also the heaviest common roofing material, so the structure underneath, rafters and decking, often needs engineering review and reinforcement. That step is frequently missing from budget quotes, and it's not optional.

What actually determines whether a slate roof reaches its rated life is the flashing, not the slate. Slate this durable has to be flashed in copper; steel or aluminum will fail decades earlier, meaning sound slate gets torn up just to replace flashing that was never going to last as long as the stone around it.

What goes wrong

Galvanized nails instead of copper or stainless corrode and let slates slide loose within years, not decades. Nail holes punched too close to the edge crack the slate under thermal stress. Foot traffic during unrelated work cracks slates that go unnoticed until a leak shows up somewhere else entirely.

How to tell good work from bad

Consistent coursing and exposure across the roof. Copper flashing at every valley, chimney, and penetration, ask specifically what metal is used. Hip and ridge slates lapped and mechanically fastened. Nothing visibly slipped, cracked, or mismatched.

Lifespan75 to 100+ yrs
Biggest cost driverStructure + copper flashing
Common failureWrong nail / wrong flashing metal

Why slate quotes vary so much. Two bids for "the same roof" can differ by tens of thousands of dollars because one includes structural reinforcement and copper flashing throughout, and the other doesn't, until the framing inspection or a failed flashing at year twenty proves it.
